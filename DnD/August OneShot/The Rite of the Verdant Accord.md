# The Rite of the Verdant Accord — Ritual, Components & Sabotage

← [[00 - Index]] | Related: [[The Gardener]], [[The Amber Company]], [[Parveen]], [[The Merchant Council of Seven]], [[Locations/The Grand Serai|The Grand Serai]]

---

> [!info]- DM Cliffnotes
> - The Rite is a legitimate, once-per-city-lifetime petition to the Seelie Throne for a boon — it is **not** inherently dangerous. It was corrupted, not cursed by nature.
> - **Five components** were required. [[The Amber Company]] substituted **three** of them with near-perfect counterfeits, each carrying trace cold iron — the one material that poisons a fae bargain instead of just blocking it.
> - The Company didn't guess. **Harun al-Noor** identified Issyldir as a target using 90 years of legitimate archive access (see [[The Amber Company]]) plus prior field experience with a smaller, contained working elsewhere.
> - The corruption didn't just fail the ritual — it kept the door open and widened it, so instead of a bounded favor from a named Court aspect, an unbound fragment of the Feywild itself (**[[The Gardener]]**) came through.
> - Use this document as the DM's answer key. Players should only assemble this picture gradually, through [[Locations/The Amber House|The Amber House]], [[Locations/Bayt al-Hikma|Bayt al-Hikma]], and [[Parveen]]'s testimony — see [[Clue Trail]].

## What the Rite Actually Is

Issyldir's Rite of the Verdant Accord is a petition ritual — a formal, one-time request for a boon from the Seelie Throne, invoked by speaking and inscribing one true Court-Name out of the Ninety-Nine that the Throne is known by. It is old magic, older than Issyldir, practiced at threshold sites all along the old silk roads by scholars who understood that the Feywild presses close to the world in certain places and that its Courts can be petitioned — carefully, honestly, and exactly once per working, since a Court that has been asked rarely grants the same favor to the same petitioner twice.

It is not inherently a horror. Performed correctly, it is closer to a very formal, very risky trade negotiation with a partner who cannot lie but also cannot be trusted to care about the small print.

**Why Issyldir could attempt it at all**: the city was founded at the confluence of three ley lines and a deep aquifer spring (see [[Locations/The Grand Serai|The Grand Serai]] and [[Locations/Hajia al-Kawthar|Hajia al-Kawthar]]) — exactly the kind of thin place the Feywild presses against. The founding families built the ritual chamber into the Grand Serai for this reason and did not use it for sixty years, arguing the whole time about whether it should ever be used at all.

## The Legitimate Components

| # | Component | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Silver stylus** | Never iron — iron severs a fae bargain before it can be spoken. The stylus inscribes the Court-Name glyph. |
| 2 | **Rose-pressed vellum** | Paper pressed from the petals of Issyldir's founding rose. Carries the city's own claim of standing before the Throne. |
| 3 | **Flower ink** | Ink milled from crushed hearth-iris and saffron — "the language of flowers," the only ink a Court-Name will hold still for. |
| 4 | **The Court-Gift** | A tribute of genuine value, given freely and without coercion — in Issyldir's case, a flawless pearl carried up the silk road three generations earlier for exactly this purpose. Fae bargains are transactions of *meaning*, not price; a gift given under duress or deception is worse than no gift at all. |
| 5 | **The Glyph Primer** | A reference text — one page per Court-Name, showing the exact stroke order for the Ninety-Nine glyphs — so the officiant copies the true Court-Name rather than composing it from memory. Issyldir's copy was a family heirloom of [[Parveen|Parveen's]] household, the al-Hikma scholars, kept in [[Bayt al-Hikma]] for three generations. |

**The invoked name**: *the Verdant Providence* — a Court-Name meaning, roughly, "she who lets no harvest fail." Chosen because it was the only Court-Name in Parveen's primer that plausibly answered the Council's stated need: prosperity, during a trade war.

**Officiant and timing**: performed by the city's most senior ritual scholar, at dusk, at the ley-line confluence beneath the Grand Serai — thresholds of time and place both mattering to a Court that measures obligation in symmetry.

## How the Amber Company Knew

The Amber Company did not stumble onto Issyldir's ritual chamber. They built toward it for decades.

- **Ninety years of legitimate access.** The Company's original charter with Issyldir (see [[Locations/The Amber House|The Amber House]]) included archive reading rights, negotiated as an ordinary trade courtesy. Over generations, Company factors quietly copied and catalogued everything the al-Hikma family had written about the Rite — including the existence and location of the Glyph Primer.
- **A prior working.** Company leadership's shift toward "longer-horizon operations" roughly forty years ago (see [[The Amber Company]]) followed a smaller, contained fae-bleed the Company had engineered — and successfully profited from — in a river city two years' travel north. That operation is deliberately left undocumented in the archive Yasmeen and Harun had access to; it exists in Company records the players never see. It is the reason the Company believed a *controlled* Bloom was possible at all. **DM note**: this is a deliberate thread left loose for a follow-on campaign.
- **Harun al-Noor's specialty.** Harun was trained in what the Company privately calls "Esoteric Contracts" — identifying cities built on ley-line confluences and cataloguing what rituals their founders left half-finished. Issyldir, water-stressed and trade-embarrassed, was simply the best-prepared candidate he had ever found: a working chamber, a documented Court-Name, and a Council desperate enough not to ask too many questions about a timely "gift."

Harun spent three years embedded as a routine factor before the ritual, cultivating the Council's trust specifically so the substituted components would arrive as an unremarkable act of goodwill. See [[The Amber Company]] for his full profile.

## The Corrupted Components — What Was Actually Substituted

The Amber Company did not need to corrupt everything. Fae bargains are precise; a working can be poisoned with a small, careful wrongness. Three substitutions were enough.

| Legitimate Component | Substituted With | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Silver stylus | A silver-plated stylus with an iron core | Iron entered the working through the officiant's own hand, at the moment of inscription — the most direct possible contamination. |
| Flower ink | A visually identical batch cut with iron gall | Iron gall ink is a real, common ink the Company could source without suspicion. Diluted into the flower ink, it was undetectable to the eye and to any check below **DC 14 Arcana**. |
| The Glyph Primer | A near-perfect forged replacement page | One stroke of the Verdant Providence's glyph was subtly altered — enough that a scholar copying it faithfully would inscribe a malformed glyph instead of the true Court-Name. The original page, along with Harun's working notes on exactly which stroke to change, can still be found in [[Locations/The Amber House|The Amber House]]'s archive. |

The rose-pressed vellum and the Court-Gift pearl were left untouched — genuine components, delivered honestly, specifically so nothing in the shipment would test as wrong under casual inspection. The sabotage lived entirely in the three items a scholar would trust without re-checking: the tool, the ink, and the reference text.

## Why It Tore Open Instead of Simply Failing

Cold iron does not just block fae magic — introduced mid-bargain, in small enough doses to go unnoticed, it curdles the exchange instead of severing it cleanly. A bargain offered to a *specific* Court-Name, written in a *malformed* glyph, using an ink and stylus both carrying iron, never reaches the Verdant Providence at all. The petition dissolves before it can be answered — but the door it opened does not close. It widens, indiscriminately, past the narrow shape a proper petition would have kept it to.

What came through was not a boon and not a punishment. It was not addressed to anyone. **[[The Gardener]]** — an unbound fragment of the Feywild's own endless growth, belonging to no Court and answering to no Court-Name — simply fell through the widened tear, the way water finds a crack.

[[Parveen]] recognized, mid-ritual, that the glyph beneath her stylus was wrong. She could not stop the working without abandoning it entirely mid-invocation, which every text she knew warned was worse than finishing badly. She finished it and inserted herself as a second anchor instead, trying to contain what she could already feel going wrong. Afterward, once she understood exactly what had been substituted, she went through every surviving copy of the ritual documentation in [[Bayt al-Hikma]] and crossed out the malformed glyph by hand — not to hide her mistake, but so no one could ever copy it again by accident. That crossed-out mark is what players find in the archives (see [[Encounters/Encounter 2 - Bayt al-Hikma|Encounter 2]]).
