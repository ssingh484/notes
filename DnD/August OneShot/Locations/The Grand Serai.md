## Overview

The Grand Serai is the seat of civic power in [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]] — the palace where the Merchant Council of Seven held court, granted audiences, sealed trade agreements, and administered the Kafil permit system. It is now the Bloom's densest point in the city, the location of the ritual chamber, and where [[Parveen|Head Scholar Parveen]] has been seated for 18 months.

**Current State**: Fully Bloom-integrated. The Council is still here. The doors cannot close.

---

## History

The Grand Serai was built 80 years ago as a deliberate statement of civic power by the third generation of Merchant Council members, who had grown up with wealth and wanted architecture that matched their ambitions. It is the largest building in Issyldir and the most ornate — the famous arabesque tilework on its floors was commissioned from artisans who traveled for three months to lay it.

The building was designed around the ritual chamber. The Council had known, from the city's founding, that [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]] sat on an unusual confluence of ley lines — the same confluence that fed the spring under [[Hajia al-Kawthar|Hajia al-Kawthar]] and the aquifer under [[Garden of Whispers|Bustan al-Asrar]]. The founding families commissioned a ritual of abundance — the Rite of the Verdant Accord — as a one-time consecration of the city's water supply. The ritual chamber was built into the palace for exactly this purpose, and never used for the first 60 years while arguments about how, when, and under what authority it should be invoked continued.

**The Tariq al-Shams decision**: Council Head Tariq al-Shams, 18 months ago, called for the ritual. The trading city had experienced three consecutive poor years. The groundwater level was dropping. He wanted abundance secured through the fae bargain available to them. He accepted the Amber Company's donated components as a gesture of reconciliation after a trade dispute. He never asked what exactly had been substituted.

**His expression in Nasr's memory**: [[Nasr|Nasr's]] vision shows Tariq al-Shams's face at the moment the Bloom pulse began. The expression players see is surprise — not fear. He did not know. He genuinely did not know.

## Description

> **READ ALOUD (exterior, from the approach road)**: *"The Grand Serai is identifiable from the moment you enter the upper city — it's the largest structure in Issyldir and the only one with a reflecting pool in its outer courtyard. The pool is dry now, flowers growing from the cracked basin. The minaret of its ceremonial tower is the source of the corrupted adhan you've heard across the city. It sounds different now, as you approach: less music, more invitation.*
>
> *The main doors are open. They cannot close — the hinges are consumed by growth, the doors themselves pushed back against their walls by eighteen months of pressure from within. Through the open doors, you can see the famous arabesque tilework of the audience hall. It is moving. Slowly. Like breathing."*

> **READ ALOUD (audience hall, the council)**: *"They are still here. Seven seats at a long council table at the far end of the hall, and seven people in those seats — in their robes of office, with their nameplates before them, with their hands on the table's surface. They are not dead. Their eyes are open. The roots go all the way down, from their lower bodies through the chair legs and into the floor, and from the floor into the walls, and from the walls into everything.*
>
> *They are looking at you. All seven, at once, with the same attention.*
>
> *The petals come from above — you cannot see where they originate. They fall in deliberate patterns, forming words before they touch the floor, dissolving as you read them. The words say: 'I have been waiting. Come in. Sit down. You have been traveling a long time.'"*

## The Arabesque Floor — Living Map

The tilework of the Grand Serai has been integrated into [[The Gardener|the Gardener's]] root system. The tiles move in slow, subtle patterns that map the Bloom's current distribution through the city in real time.

**DC 16 Investigation or DC 14 Arcana**: The player reads the map. They can identify every survivor location, every high-density Bloom zone, and the ritual chamber's location behind the council table.

**Players with 4+ Bloom Exposure**: Read the map automatically, without a check. The city presents itself to them. This is both information and warning.

---

## The Ritual Chamber

The passage from the audience hall to the ritual chamber is partly hidden (behind the council table, through an alcove) but not sealed. The passage is thigh-deep in flowers.

> **READ ALOUD**: *"The ritual chamber was built for a single purpose: a wooden chair, raised on a small dais, surrounded by geometric inlays on the floor that your reading of the ritual documentation or scholarly training recognizes as divine invocation markers. The dais and chair have been grown around. Roots from the floor hold the chair in place; flowers grow through the arms, the back, the seat. The geometric inlays in the floor are still visible, still precise — the Bloom has grown along them, tracing their lines perfectly.*
>
> *And in the chair: [[Parveen|Parveen]]. She has had eighteen months to make peace with this room. She has had eighteen months to prepare what she needs to say to whoever eventually came through that door. She looks at you. She says: 'Sit if you like. There's nowhere to be, at this point.'"*

## Aftermath

The Grand Serai is where the adventure ends. Whichever of the three endings players choose (seal the tear, willing vessel, give the scroll to the Company), the space itself changes:

- **Seal the Tear**: The flowers in the hall brown inward from the walls. The Council slumps. The building remains Bloom-integrated but no longer growing. The tilework slows and stops. The adhan from the tower goes silent.
- **Willing Vessel**: The hall flowers brighten briefly, then settle. The vessel walks to the center of the geometric inlay and remains there. The city's flowers will begin, slowly, over years, to change — becoming less consuming, more decorative. The Serai becomes a pilgrimage site.
- **Give the Scroll**: The building remains as-is. The Company's agents arrive within a day. Players will not be present for what happens next.
