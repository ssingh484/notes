## Overview

The Bayt al-Hikma is [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir's]] 90-year-old library-palace, built and endowed by the city's first generation of permanent residents who wanted the trading city to be remembered as more than commercial. It is currently functioning as the survivor refuge managed by [[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim]], housing 23 people on its upper floors while its lower levels are consumed by Bloom growth.

**Current State**: Ground floor inaccessible (Bloom-choked). Second floor navigable but unsafe. Third floor: the survivor camp. Fourth floor: scholars' atelier, [[Nadia bint Yusuf|Nadia's]] rooms, [[Parveen|Parveen's]] study.

---

## History

The Bayt al-Hikma was built over the course of eleven years by the city's first native-born generation — people who had grown up in the trading city and wanted to leave something that wasn't commerce. The building was a deliberate cultural statement: we are not only a souq, we are a city.

The ground floor was always the civic archive — Kafil permits, trade manifests, birth and death records, property deeds. This was the administrative center of the Kafil system. The city's entire documentation of who was allowed to be here, and for how long, and for what price, existed on these shelves. It still does. The Bloom has preserved it perfectly.

The upper floors housed scholars, scribes, visiting experts, and a rotating collection of books purchased from caravans passing through. [[Parveen|Head Scholar Parveen]] ran the institution for 20 years before the Bloom. [[Nadia bint Yusuf|Nadia]] was her apprentice for the last 7 of those years.

**The endowment controversy**: The building was partially funded by [[The Amber Company|Amber Company]] precursors — a trade consortium with interests in the region. In exchange, they had reading access to the archive. This relationship was formal and disclosed. It is also, in retrospect, how the Amber Company had 140 years of access to Kafil documentation and knew exactly how many non-native workers were in the city at any given time.

## Description

> **READ ALOUD (outside the building)**: *"The Bayt al-Hikma — the House of Wisdom — has the Bloom in it the way an old wound has scar tissue: woven into the structure, not removable without damage. The lower windows are dark with root growth. The building's famous cornerstone rose-carving — the symbol of Issyldir, reproduced on every civic building — has been joined by a real rose growing from the crack beside it, as if the stone one finally took root.*
>
> *The lintel above the main door has been split by something growing through it. The words carved into the lintel — 'Enter to Learn. Leave to Teach.' — are still legible. The roots growing through the crack have framed them."*

> **READ ALOUD (third floor, arrival)**: *"The third floor is a working camp. Long wooden tables pushed together in the center of the main hall, twenty-three people at various points of occupation — some eating, some sleeping on bedrolls pushed against the walls, some doing nothing with the concentrated focus of people who have been doing nothing for eighteen months. The windows are barricaded with furniture. The air smells of bodies and bread and, underneath everything, roses. A man in a magistrate's robe — impossibly clean — stands at the far end of the room. He has been waiting for you."*

## The Records (Second Floor)

The second-floor archives contain 140 years of civic documentation. Much of it is administrative routine, but the following are findable:

## Parveen's Study (Fourth Floor)

Parveen's study is accessible, unlocked, and full of paper. It has the settled disorder of a working scholar rather than the chaos of a search — things are where she left them, 18 months ago, mid-project.

The locked drawer requires DC 14 Thieves' Tools or DC 16 STR. Inside: the original component manifest with Parveen's handwritten notes, including the margin note that makes the sabotage explicit: *"These components were not ordered by the Council. Source: Amber Company. Do not proceed."*

A small detail: on Parveen's desk, a half-finished cup of tea. It has been there for 18 months and has long since turned to dust, but the cup is still there. [[Fatima Rashid|Fatima]], if present (Present memory), will not look at it.

## The Building as Character

The Bayt al-Hikma is not just a location — it is a record of what Issyldir wanted to be. The Bloom has preserved it. The archives that document the Kafil system's injustices are perfectly intact in amber-colored root growth. The books purchased from passing caravans are still shelved, organized, labeled. [[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim]] has maintained the third floor with the same care he applies to everything — even in apocalypse, the building functions.

DMs should play the building as orderly, preserved, and quietly devastating. The horror here is not destruction. The horror is that everything important is still here, still readable, and nothing it documents has mattered for 18 months.
