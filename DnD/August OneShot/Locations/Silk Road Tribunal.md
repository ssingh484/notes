## History

The Silk Road Tribunal was Issyldir's official administrative court — the place where trade disputes were adjudicated, permit appeals were heard, and Kafil documentation was issued and renewed. For 140 years, every non-native worker who came to Issyldir passed through this building's permit window. Every appeal was filed here. Every appeal was denied here at a rate of 94%.

The building contains the city's administrative soul — orderly, documented, and utterly uninterested in the people it processed.

**Abbas** was the senior permit clerk — a position passed down through his family for two generations. His grandfather started the job. His father continued it. Abbas continued it, because the job was there and he was trained for it and it paid. He is Bloomed now, mildly, and is still processing paperwork. The paperwork is for permits of people who have been dead or gone for 18 months. He processes them anyway.

## Abbas

Abbas is middle-aged, methodical, and mildly Bloom-touched in a way that has made him patient to the point of stillness. He is processing old permit applications — working through the backlog, he would say, if asked. The applications he is processing are for workers who entered the city between 3 and 5 years ago. Their permits expired long before the Bloom. He is completing their renewals.

**DC 12 Insight**: Abbas is not confused. He knows the city is empty and these people are gone. He is completing their paperwork because it is the only thing he knows how to do that feels like it matters.

**Talking to Abbas**: He responds to direct questions with direct answers, in a professional register that does not fit the current circumstances. He will pull any file from the archive on request. He will say "please" when asking players to wait while he retrieves a file.

**What Abbas says, without prompting, after about 5 minutes of interaction**: *"Ninety-three percent of the permits I processed in my career were denied on appeal. The grounds were usually technical — wrong form, wrong sponsor, fee unpaid. The applicants usually re-filed. They usually got denied again. Most of them kept working. They didn't have another option. I approved the paperwork. I just didn't... make the rules. I didn't make the rules."*

## The Archive (Second Floor)

**DC 14 Investigation or 20 minutes with Abbas's guidance** (Abbas will guide freely if asked): Players can extract the following evidence.

| Evidence | What It Shows |
|---|---|
| Kafil denial statistics (bound annual reports) | 94% appeal denial rate over 140 years; no year ever exceeded 8% approval |
| "Exception criteria" file | The four circumstances under which a Kafil worker could be granted permanent residency. All four require Council sponsorship. No Council member has sponsored a non-native worker in the entire 140-year archive. |
| [[Rajan Pillai|Rajan's]] file | His first Kafil permit, 15 years ago. Every renewal since. Seven appeal attempts, all denied on technical grounds. A note in his file in his sponsor's handwriting: "Excellent worker. Suggest annual renewal rather than residency — loss of leverage otherwise." |
| Worker death records | Cross-referenced with the Bloom. Nine of the eleven workers [[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim]] "pruned" had active Kafil restrictions. They could not leave the city. The two native Issyldiri who were pruned had no such restriction. |

## The Core Revelation

The piece of evidence players may not immediately assemble without help: **[[Rajan Pillai|Rajan]] and most of the non-native workers could not physically leave Issyldir when the Bloom began, because leaving would void their Kafil permits and make them permanently inadmissible — an economic death sentence**. The workers who died to the Bloom were the workers who were trapped here by paperwork. The Kafil system didn't just exploit them while the city was functioning. It killed them when it failed.

If [[Rajan Pillai|Rajan]] is with the players when they find his file: he reads the note from his sponsor ("loss of leverage otherwise") twice. He folds the document. He puts it in his pocket. He does not speak for a few minutes. When he speaks: *"I knew. I think I knew. I just had nowhere to put it."*
