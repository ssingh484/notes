## The Setup

The players have navigated to the heart of [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]]. They carry the knowledge of the ritual's sabotage and [[The Amber Company|their employers' true agenda]]. Ahead: the Grand Serai, which has been sealed for 18 months, where the seven members of the Merchant Council are still seated at their table, merged into the architecture, still breathing.

**Scale**: The audience hall is approximately 80 ft × 50 ft. Each column is a 5 ft square providing half cover. The council table runs across the back third of the hall (~25 ft wide). The reflecting pool is 20 ft × 12 ft, centered in the room.

**Difficult Terrain**: The reflecting pool (submerged roots beneath still water, disorienting visuals). The area within 5 ft of the council table (dense root growth from the floor). The Ritual Chamber passage (flowers thigh-high throughout).

---

## The Approach — The Adhan

> **READ ALOUD (as players near the Serai)**: *"You hear it before you see the building. The adhan — the call to prayer — sounds from the tall minaret above the Serai's outer courtyard. You have heard this call distorted a dozen times since entering Issyldir, sounding at the wrong hour in the wrong voice. This one is different. It sounds, note for note, exactly like the adhan is supposed to sound — except the words are in a language that does not exist. You almost understand it. DC 15 Insight: the call is repeating the same phrase over and over, in a language no language has words for. [Players at 3+ Bloom Exposure hear it clearly: 'Come home. Come home. Come home.']*"

---

## Entering the Hall

> **READ ALOUD**: *"The doors to the Grand Serai are open — they cannot close now, the hinges consumed by growth, the doors themselves pushed back against the walls by the weight of what came through them. You step into the audience hall and stop.*
>
> *The reflecting pool is still there, and it still holds water, and it is still beautiful. Everything else has changed. The arched colonnades are wrapped in vines to their capitals. The floor — the famous arabesque tilework — is still perfectly visible, except that it is moving. Slowly. Like breathing. Like a map being revised in real time.*
>
> *At the far end of the hall, still seated at their council table, the seven merchants of Issyldir sit in their chairs. They are not dead. Their eyes are open. Their roots go all the way down.*
>
> *The scent of the room is roses and something older, something that doesn't have a name in any language you know. And from somewhere above you, from a place you cannot see, petals fall. They form words on the floor as they land: 'You're here. I've been tending your seeds. Sit. Rest. Let it grow.'"*

---

## The Reflecting Pool
Any player who looks into the reflecting pool sees themselves, wearing flowers, looking peaceful. **Players with 2+ [[Bloom Exposure Mechanic|Bloom Exposure]]**: must succeed on a DC 13 WIS save or gain 1 additional Bloom Exposure point.

---

## The Floor — The Living Map
**Investigation DC 16 or Arcana DC 14**: The arabesque tilework is a real-time diagram of the Bloom's roots through the city — a living map. On success, the player can identify every survivor location, every Bloom-dense zone, and (most importantly) the exact location of [[Parveen|Parveen's]] body in the Ritual Chamber.

**Players at 4+ Bloom Exposure**: Read the map automatically, no check required.

---

## The Stage 3 Reveal — The Seeds

[[The Gardener]] addresses each player by name. It knows them. It references the specific seed planted in each character — the dream, the trinket, the moment of inexplicable beauty. (DM: prepare these privately for each player beforehand; see [[The Gardener]] for guidance.)

> *"[Player name]. You carried the saffron from the merchant in [hometown] two winters ago. You remember the smell. You thought of it again last spring. That was me. I have been watching over you for a long time. You are already mine. Come home."*

**Wisdom ST DC 15** for each player who receives this: on a failure, the player gains 1 Bloom Exposure point. On a success, no effect — but the DM notes that the character now knows the Gardener has been watching them specifically.

---

## The Gardener's Dialogue

See [[The Gardener]] for the full dialogue breakdown. Key beats:

1. **On entry**: *"You're here. I've been tending your seeds. Sit. Rest. Let it grow."*
2. **When players move toward the Ritual Chamber**: *"She chose this. She is content."* (Not entirely true. [[Parveen]] will say so, bluntly.)
3. **On the Sealed Scroll**: *"It is hers. She has always been free to give it."*
4. **If players show hostility**: The Gardener does not understand hostility here. It tries again, then tries the personal seed reveal. It does not comprehend rejection.

---

## Finding Parveen

The Ritual Chamber is accessed through a passage behind the council table — partially hidden but not sealed. Players who search find it automatically. The passage is thigh-deep in flowers.

> **READ ALOUD (Ritual Chamber)**: *"She is here. She is sitting in a carved wooden chair — she was sitting in it eighteen months ago, and the chair and the floor have grown around her since, roots from her lower back connecting her to the earth, flowers growing through her shoulders and forearms. Her face is her own. Her hands are her own. She is looking at you with the patient attention of someone who has had eighteen months to practice patience.*
>
> *'You're late,' she says. 'But you're here. Ask your questions.'"*

**[[Fatima Rashid|Fatima's]] reaction**: If Fatima is present (and possibly in "Before" memory), arriving in this room breaks her into "Present" memory permanently for the rest of the session. The scene between Fatima and Parveen should be played out without time pressure. It is the emotional core of the adventure.

## The Three Endings

### Ending 1: Seal the Tear
**Trigger**: Players choose to destroy the Sealed Scroll.

[[Parveen]] explains the sealing ritual. She can perform it herself, but it requires the scroll to be removed from her ribcage first. This is a profoundly difficult moment — the removal requires a healer or anyone with a steady hand (Medicine DC 14 or [[Parveen]] guides them through it). The scroll, once removed, is a dense document written in sacred script on linen paper.

The sealing ritual takes 1 minute of concentration while holding the scroll. [[The Gardener]] does not interrupt — it watches through [[Nasr]], confused but non-violent. At the ritual's completion, the scroll burns to ash without flame. The petals in the room begin to brown. [[The Gardener]] retreats through the tear.

**Cost**: [[Parveen]] dies within hours of the scroll's destruction. The Bloom stops spreading. The city remains corrupted — no longer growing, never healing. [[The Amber Company]] receives nothing. Players leave knowing the truth but without the scroll as proof.

### Ending 2: Willing Vessel
**Trigger**: Players propose or accept an offer to find a willing vessel for [[The Gardener]].

[[Parveen]] explains what a vessel means: the person accepts the Gardener's full presence, containing it, and walks it back through the tear. The vessel is not killed. They are transformed beyond recognition of self. They remain in the Serai permanently. The city heals slowly over years.

**Vessel candidates** (each should be played out as a roleplay moment):
- **[[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim]]**: *"It seems like appropriate penance."* His offer is quiet and without self-pity.
- **[[Nadia bint Yusuf|Nadia]]**: *"I've been going there in my dreams for two months. Maybe I'm already partway there."* Her offer is curious more than afraid.
- **[[Rajan Pillai|Rajan]]**: *"I have nowhere else to go anyway."* Three beats of silence. *"And this city built me. I suppose I might as well stay in it."* His offer is the hardest to witness.

### Ending 3: Give the Scroll to the Company
**Trigger**: Players choose to extract the scroll and return to [[Saleh]].

No dramatic moment prevents this. [[Parveen]] says, simply: *"I know what they will do with it. I hope you do too."* The scroll can be taken. [[Saleh]] receives it with genuine relief. The long-form implication: [[The Amber Company]] will attempt re-invocation within the year, aimed at a rival city.

## If Combat Triggers

**Trigger**: Players attack the Council table, attempt to destroy nodes aggressively, or try to force their way to [[Parveen]] through the council.

The [[Bloom Council]] activates. See [[Bloom Council]] for the full stat block and encounter mechanics.

### Encounter Setup
| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Enemy | [[Bloom Council]] — 7 nodes, each targetable individually |
| Victory condition | Destroy 3 of 7 nodes |
| Environment | The audience hall (see map above) |
| Key hazard | Bloom Exposure pulses when nodes are destroyed |
| Legendary actions | 3/round, taken at end of other creatures' turns |

### Environment Hazards (Rolling per round)
At the start of each combat round, roll a d6:
- **1–2**: Nothing extra
- **3–4**: The tilework shifts in a 10 ft zone (DM's choice of location) — difficult terrain for 1 round
- **5–6**: The reflecting pool surface ripples; any creature within 10 ft must succeed DC 12 WIS save or be **distracted** (loses its reaction until the start of its next turn)

### After Combat
If the players defeat the Bloom Council (destroy 3 nodes), the entity's manifestation weakens enough for [[Parveen]] to speak freely. [[The Gardener]] does not attack again. The three endings remain available.

> **READ ALOUD (After Combat Victory)**: *"The three collapsed Council members — whatever they were, whatever remained of them — slump in their chairs. The flowers around them go still. The room smells like rain. From behind the council table, from the hidden chamber, a voice: 'Are you hurt? Come through. I've been waiting.'"*

---

## Final Beat

Whichever ending players choose, the last thing they experience as they leave the Serai:

> *"The flowers brown at the edges. Not all at once — from the walls inward, from the highest blooms downward, a slow withdrawal. Petals fall in the air around you. They do not form words this time. But the scent remains — roses and earth and something ancient — and just before it is gone entirely, you catch one last thing:*
>
> *'I only wanted you to grow.'*
>
> *Whether that is tragic or terrifying — you will not agree on the answer."*
