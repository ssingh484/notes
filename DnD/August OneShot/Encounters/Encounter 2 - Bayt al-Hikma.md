## The Setup

The Bayt al-Hikma is a 90-year-old library-palace, half-consumed by Bloom roots from the ground floor up. The third floor is clear enough to function as a survivor camp. [[Kasim al-Nuri]] has maintained 23 people here for 18 months through a process he calls "pruning" — identifying and eliminating Bloomed individuals before they spread. Tomorrow, [[Nadia bint Yusuf]] is scheduled.

## Ground Floor — Entry

> **READ ALOUD**: *"The Bayt al-Hikma shows you its damage from the outside: the lower windows are dark with root growth, the carved stone lintel above the main door has been split by something growing through it, and the rose carved into the cornerstone — the city's symbol, ubiquitous in Issyldir — has been joined by a real rose growing from the crack beside it, as if the stone one has finally taken root. Inside the ground floor, you can barely move for the vegetation. The shelves are still there beneath it, records still in their labeled slots, perfectly preserved inside a cage of flowers. The staircase up is clear. Someone has been maintaining it — there are fresh scuff marks on the steps. Up above, you can hear voices."*

---

## Third Floor — The Survivor Camp

> **READ ALOUD**: *"The third floor is a functioning camp — barely, but functioning. Long wooden tables pushed together in the center of the main hall, survivors seated or standing at them, looking up as you arrive. Twenty-three people, you count, ranging in age from perhaps sixteen to well past sixty. Some of them look at you with cautious hope. Some of them do not look at you at all. A tall man in a magistrate's robe — clean, somehow, impossibly clean after eighteen months — stands at the far end of the room with the posture of someone who has been in charge for a long time and has made his peace with the weight of it. 'I am Kasim al-Nuri,' he says. 'You are the first people from outside in eight months. Welcome to what's left.'"*

## The Social Encounter — Kasim

[[Kasim al-Nuri]] will conduct a formal interview. He wants to know: who sent the players, what they want, and whether they represent a threat. He is scrupulously fair and direct. He does not lie. He omits.

### Key Questions and Answers

**"Who sent you?"** → Kasim knows [[The Amber Company]] by reputation. His expression tightens slightly. *"They are not a humanitarian organization. Whatever they told you about their reasons — consider it carefully."* He does not elaborate unprompted.

**"Who are all these people?"** → He gives a general account: survivors of the initial Bloom pulse, workers who couldn't leave, a few scholars. He refers to [[Rajan Pillai|Rajan's]] group as "the workers" without names or acknowledgement.

**"What is the pruning?"** → He explains the methodology calmly and clearly, including the criteria: behavioral changes, perception anomalies, progressive loss of agency. He does not mention [[Nadia bint Yusuf|Nadia]] unless asked.

**"What about Nadia?"** → If players ask or learn about Nadia from other survivors, Kasim confirms she is showing signs. *"The dreams she describes are consistent with progressive Bloom infection. I have seen it before. The timeline is clear."* He will listen to counter-arguments with genuine attention. He is not immune to evidence.

### The Pattern

**Investigation DC 14** (examining the notebook if players get access to it, or cross-referencing survivor accounts): Nine of eleven pruned individuals were non-native workers. Two native Issyldiri were on the list; both had overt behavioral symptoms the non-native individuals did not exhibit.

**If Rajan is present with high trust**: He points this out quietly. No check needed. *"I have been counting. Of eleven. Nine were workers."*

**Confronting Kasim with the pattern**: See [[Kasim al-Nuri]] NPC notes for the full roleplay guidance. This is the encounter's emotional core.

## The Records — What Players Can Find

### Second Floor Records (Investigation DC varies)
All records require navigating the second floor, which has moderate Bloom growth (roots at baseboards, flowers from window frames — walkable but unsettling).

| Document | DC | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Ritual documentation (partial) | 12 | The Rite of the Bountiful Name invoked *Al-Razzaq*. The name has been crossed out in every copy. |
| Crossed-out name (legible) | 15 | The crossed-out name — the actual ritual content. Arcana DC 13: the substituted marks are a corruption glyph, not a divine name. |
| Amber Company manifest | 11 | Components delivered 19 months ago as "gift of reconciliation." Items cross-referenced: Arcana DC 13 to identify the substitutions as unusual. |
| **The Ledger** | 10 | Every Kafil permit issued in Issyldir's 140-year history. Every PC's name or hometown appears in connection with a trade good. **Stage 2 Reveal.** |
| Parveen's correspondence file | 14 | Letters from [[Parveen]] objecting to the ritual over a period of six months. The final letter: sealed, dated the night of the ritual, addressed to the Council. Unopened. |

### Fourth Floor — Nadia's Room
Players can access this with Nadia's permission or by searching while she's occupied elsewhere.

**Nadia's journal** (no check required if she gives it freely, DC 13 Investigation to find without her): 23 entries of dream descriptions. Entry 11 describes being a root moving through the city. Entry 19 describes a woman in the palace in pain. Entry 23, written three days ago: *"The new people are coming. I can feel them from here."*

**Parveen's Study** (accessible, papers everywhere, mostly ritual scholarship):
- A complete copy of the Rite of the Bountiful Name in its legitimate form
- Handwritten notes on the 99 divine names, *Al-Razzaq* extensively annotated
- A locked drawer (DC 14 Thieves' Tools or DC 16 STR): inside, the original component manifest with Parveen's handwritten margin notes identifying each ingredient. One margin note: *"These components were not ordered by the Council. Source: Amber Company. Do not proceed."* This document makes the sabotage explicit.

## If Combat Triggers

**Trigger**: Players attempt to protect Nadia from being taken for pruning, are caught in Kasim's study reading the notebook, or directly accuse Kasim in a way he interprets as a threat to his authority.

**Kasim's Response**: He does not immediately escalate to violence. He says: *"This is a difficult situation. I would ask you to step back and discuss this."* Only if players continue to escalate does he signal his lieutenants.

### Combat Roster

| Creature | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [[Kasim al-Nuri (Combat)]] | 1 | Uses tactical positioning; Advantage on attacks vs. Bloom-exposed targets |
| [[Kasim's Lieutenant]] | 3 | Position: between Kasim and the stairwell; will protect the stairwell |

**19 civilian survivors** are present. They will flee to the eastern end of the room and huddle. 1d4 of them may attempt to intervene — rolling to aid Kasim or the players depending on individual relationships built during the scene.

**Environment**:
- Long tables as cover (half cover if prone behind them)
- Windows barricaded with furniture (can be broken: DC 14 STR; outside is a 40 ft drop)
- Kasim's office door can be barricaded from inside (slows movement, does not stop it)

**Victory conditions**:
- Incapacitating (not killing) Kasim ends the combat — his lieutenants stand down
- Killing Kasim: the surviving civilians lose their organizational center; Nadia is freed but 23 people now have no leadership structure
- Talking Kasim down (DC 15 Persuasion using any discovered evidence of the Bloom pattern): he stands down, visibly shaken. He becomes a potential vessel candidate.

> [!tip] What Kasim Wants to Happen
> Kasim does not want to fight the players. He wants them to accept his judgment. If players engage him seriously — treat his arguments with respect, present counter-evidence carefully — he will respond in kind. The combat trigger is a last resort, not an inevitability.
