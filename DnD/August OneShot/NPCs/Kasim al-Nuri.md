## Description

**Age**: 55. Tall, silver-haired, the posture of a man who once commanded rooms and still does. His magistrate's robe is still clean — he has been maintaining it for 18 months out of pure habit. The discipline of it is, itself, a kind of horror.

His left hand is covered by a leather glove with a small slit cut along the back — just enough for the blue flower growing through his palm to breathe without being visible. The glove is always on. He is never without it.

He keeps a small leather notebook. On one side: 23 names in neat script — his surviving charges. On the other side: 11 names, each crossed through with a single precise line. He will not show this to anyone. He will not discard it either.

---

## Personality

Measured, deliberate, full sentences. He listens with total attention and responds to exactly what was said — not what he wanted to hear, not a deflection, but precisely what was said. Under any other circumstances, this would be comforting. Here it makes him harder to read, because his careful listening is itself a practiced tool.

He believes, genuinely and without performance, that what he has done was necessary. He has never looked at the 11 names from above. He has reasons for each one that are defensible in isolation.

---

## Backstory

Third-generation Issyldiri. The al-Nuri family name means something in [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]] — or it did. His grandfather was among the founding merchant families. Kasim became a magistrate because he believed the law was essentially just and that careful application of it would produce fair outcomes. He was known for patience, for listening fully, for reasoned verdicts. He was considered one of Issyldir's better magistrates.

**His complication**: He enforced the **Kafil system** because the law required it. He returned workers to sponsors who mistreated them because the law required it. He issued exit denials because the law required it. He always felt this was unfortunate but necessary — the system had to be maintained to function. He never noticed how much work "unfortunate but necessary" was doing.

**Night of the ritual**: He was at the [[Bayt al-Hikma|Bayt al-Hikma]], considering whether to put his magistrate's seal on [[Parveen|Parveen's]] letter of objection, when the ritual went wrong. He survived because the library's stone walls dampened the initial pulse. He watched from a high window. He decided, in the first hour, that someone had to be in charge. He was the most senior surviving authority figure. He was not wrong about this.

---

## The Pattern

Of the 11 people Kasim has pruned, **9 were non-native workers**. None were native Issyldiri. The two native-born people on the list both had overt behavioral symptoms; the workers who were pruned had subtler or no symptoms.

He has reasons for each decision that are individually defensible. He has not looked at the pattern from above. He does not know the pattern exists. He is not lying about this.

> [!warning] Confronting Kasim With the Pattern
> If players show him evidence of the pattern, Kasim goes very still. Then: "That is not — I would not—" He stops. He looks at the notebook. Long silence. Then: "I need to look at this more carefully." He becomes either an ally (if players are gentle and give him space to reckon) or defensive and dangerous (if players treat him as a monster). He is neither innocent nor monstrous. Handle with care.

---

## The Flower

He found it growing through his left palm four months ago. He has been hiding it. He tells himself it has not changed his cognition. He may be right. He may not be.

The flower is a dark blue, five-petaled variety that does not exist in nature outside of [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir's]] current state. It is slightly warm to the touch. It is beautiful.

## Knowledge

Kasim knows:
- The full layout of the [[Bayt al-Hikma]] and its current survivor situation
- The broad outline of what happened at the ritual (witnessed from a window)
- That [[Parveen]] was last seen entering the [[The Grand Serai|Grand Serai]] on the night of the ritual
- The structural weaknesses of various Bloom-affected zones

Kasim does **not** know:
- That the ritual was deliberately sabotaged by [[The Amber Company]]
- The specific details of the Rite of the Verdant Accord
- That his Bloom is affecting his judgment

## Role-Play Notes

**When players arrive at the Bayt al-Hikma**: He receives them formally, with the same courtesy he'd extend to any official party. He introduces the survivor situation clearly and honestly — except he does not mention [[Rajan Pillai|Rajan]] by name and refers to "the workers" as a category.

**When players ask about the pruning**: He explains the methodology with the same patient, careful logic he'd apply to a legal judgment. He will not be defensive unless challenged on the pattern.

**When players want to protect Nadia**: He listens. He considers. He says: "I understand your concern. She is showing signs. I have made this judgment before. The alternative is—" He stops. "The alternative is everyone."

**If combat is triggered**: See [[Kasim al-Nuri (Combat)]] for full stat block.

**As a Vessel candidate (Encounter 3)**: If players have built trust with Kasim and shown him the pattern, he may volunteer as the willing vessel — partly out of genuine desire to fix what he has done, partly out of guilt, and partly because leading this group has been destroying him for 18 months. His offer is quiet. *"It seems like appropriate penance."*
