## Description

**Age**: 58. Broad, unhurried, large hands. Close-trimmed white beard. He moves through [[The Bakhoor House|his shop]] the way people move through very familiar spaces — without looking, without thinking, without hurry. He has been here for 22 years. His hands smell permanently of oud: warm, woody, deep. Several players will notice this before they notice anything else about him.

---

## Personality

Slowly. With weight. When he speaks, it lands. He says fewer words than most people and more is carried by each one. He has the unhurried quality of a man who has made his peace with waiting.

He calls everyone *"ya walad"* (my child) or *"ya binti"* (my daughter) regardless of their age — a term of address from his grandmother's region. He extends this to people he trusts, which is rare. When he uses it with the players, it means something.

He offers gahwa (cardamom coffee) and dates to every visitor by reflex. He will be quietly, genuinely offended if this is refused. It is not about the food. It is about the tradition of being received as a guest — a tradition the city is forgetting.

---

## Backstory

*"Al-Sharq"* means "of the East" — a geographic identifier his grandfather adopted on emigrating west. Three generations as a family name and it still announces, to anyone who knows, that his family came from somewhere else. He has lived in [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]] for 22 years. He considers this home. The city has never fully agreed.

He came as a master perfumer and bakhoor blender, hired by a merchant house. When that house "restructured" (the owner's son replaced the old staff), he opened his own back-alley shop. He has been there 22 years. His wife and two sons live in the eastern city of Kadar. He sends money when he can. He cannot anymore.

---

## The Discovery

In the Bloom's first week, Hamid fought the floral scent the way a perfumer fights an overpowering note — by layering heavier woods underneath it. He found a specific blend: oud-heavy, frankincense cut with clove, burned at a controlled heat. He prepares each batch with a personal prayer from his grandmother's tradition, spoken over the coals before he adds the blend.

He suspects the prayer is part of the formula. He is probably right. Belief, in this city, has mechanical weight. He has never tested the blend without the prayer because he does not want to know the answer.

---

## What He Has Not Said

He recognizes the substituted ritual components. He handles similar unusual compounds as scent-base experiments in his work — the chemical compositions are close enough to familiar compounds that he understood what was in the manifest when he read the library records. He knows what was in those crates. He knows what they would do.

He has not told anyone because **the last two people who tried to share similar information were placed on [[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim's]] pruning consideration list**. He is waiting for someone to ask the right question.

> [!tip] The Right Question
> *"Do you know anything about what was in those ritual components?"* or *"You've handled unusual compounds — do any of these ingredient names mean anything to you?"*
>
> If players ask this question, Hamid goes still for a moment, looks at his hands, and then speaks for about 10 uninterrupted minutes. He has been waiting 18 months to tell someone this.

---

## The Back Room

Behind a curtain: a portrait of his wife and two sons. A prayer mat worn smooth from 22 years of use. A locked wooden box with a flower growing from the keyhole — it appeared three months ago. It has not spread further. He checks it every morning with the careful attention of a man who has spent his life understanding how things grow. He has not yet decided what it means.

---

## Role-Play Notes

**On first meeting**: He looks up from a jar without surprise. He has been expecting someone eventually. He prepares gahwa immediately. The offer is not negotiable.

**When players ask about the city**: He gives information in the form of observations, not advice. He does not tell players what to do.

**On [[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim]]**: *"Kasim kept people alive. That is true. I do not go to see him because I am not sure which people he has kept."* Said with no particular emotion.

**On 22 years in Issyldir**: *"My sons have never been here. I have sent them money from here for twenty-two years. My oldest wants to be a perfumer. He has never smelled oud. Every time I think about this, I burn more wood."*
