## Overview

The Merchant Council of Seven was [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir's]] governing body for 140 years — seven seats, filled by the city's dominant trade families, with authority over permits, trade agreements, taxation, and civic development. They administered the Kafil system. They commissioned the Grand Serai. They approved the ritual.

They are all still in the Serai. All seven.

---

## Individual Members

### Tariq al-Shams — Council Head (Seat T, Center)
The peacock throne. Family business: gem trade and luxury goods. The most senior Council member at the time of the ritual — had held his seat for 22 years.

**Background**: Tariq called for the ritual in good faith. Three bad years, dropping groundwater, a city that needed a reason to believe in its own future. He accepted the Amber Company's components as a reconciliation gesture after a trade dispute that had embarrassed the Company publicly. He didn't verify them.

**His expression in [[Nasr|Nasr's]] vision**: Surprise. Not fear, not guilt — surprise. He genuinely didn't know. This is one of the adventure's most morally complicated facts: the person most responsible for the ritual decision did not intend the outcome.

**Merged state**: Most completely integrated. His roots go deeper than anyone else's. The peacock throne has become him. As a [[Bloom Council|Bloom Council]] node, his seat has +2 to all saves.

---

### Zainab al-Harir — Seat 1 (leftmost)
Family business: silk and textile import. The Council's youngest senior member at 45.

**Background**: The only Council member who voted against the ritual. She submitted a written objection: *"We do not understand what we are invoking and we have not adequately studied the components."* The vote was 6-1. Her objection is in the [[Bayt al-Hikma|archive]].

**Merged state**: Her expression in the council chair is what players might interpret as fury, or effort. She is fighting the collective mind. In Bloom Council combat, her node is the one that can speak: a **DC 14 Wisdom check** by any player within 10 ft. allows them to hear her say one word before the Bloom suppresses the communication. The word is "sabotage." Players who hear this and act on it — approaching the council table rather than destroying it — may find a way to resolve the encounter without combat.

---

### Dawud al-Baharat — Seat 2
Family business: spice trade. Had the longest relationship with the Amber Company of any Council member.

**Background**: Dawud was the relationship manager for the Company deal — the person who vouched for the components being a legitimate gift. He knew less than he should have. He trusted more than he should have. He is the Council member who feels most like a cautionary tale.

**Merged state**: Face almost serene, which players find more disturbing than the others.

---

### Unnamed Members — Seats 3, 4, 5, 6
The remaining four Council members are not named in the adventure's recorded history. Players may discover their names in the archive if they look. They are Bloom Council nodes with no special traits.

## As The Bloom Council

See [[Bloom Council]] for full encounter mechanics. In brief:

- Destroy 3 of 7 to end the encounter
- Each destroyed node triggers a Bloom Exposure pulse (DC 15 CON)
- [[Bloom Council#Individual Node Profiles|Zainab's special trait]] can allow the encounter to end without full combat

## What Happened to Them

All seven Council members were in the Grand Serai for an emergency session regarding the ritual outcome when the Bloom pulse expanded from the ritual chamber. They were the first people in Issyldir to be Bloom-touched at the highest intensity. The Gardener's entry through the ritual-anchored space merged them to the architecture within the first 24 hours.

They have been there for 18 months. Their bodies are alive. Whether they are present in any meaningful sense varies by the Council member — Zainab appears to be fighting; Tariq appears to be gone; the others are unclear.

**If players ask [[Parveen]] about them**: *"I don't know if Tariq is still in there. I think Zainab might be. I have been sitting in this chair for eighteen months and I have had a great deal of time to wonder about it."*
