## Overview

The Amber Company is the adventure's true antagonist — not the [[The Gardener|Gardener]], not [[Kasim al-Nuri|Kasim]], but the mercantile organization that engineered the Bloom event and then hired the players to retrieve the one artifact that would let them do it again. The Company is not evil in the melodramatic sense: they are rational actors applying commercial logic to situations where commercial logic produces catastrophic results.

**Their Agenda**: Develop the capacity to deploy targeted Bloom events in rival cities, then sell "restoration services" to affected populations. The Sealed Scroll, properly reverse-engineered, gives them the ritual infrastructure they need.

---

## History

The Amber Company has operated in the region for 110 years — predating most of the cities they trade in. They began as a spice consortium, expanded into precious metals, and over time developed a specialization in what they call "transition services": helping wealthy parties navigate political disruptions, city changes, and power vacuums.

Their 90-year presence in [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]] was legitimate. They had real commercial relationships. They knew the city's archive better than most residents. The relationship turned predatory approximately 40 years ago when a new Company leadership cohort began planning longer-horizon operations.

**The Issyldir Operation**: Three years of preparation. Harun al-Noor embedded in the city as a routine factor, building the relationship with the Merchant Council, offering the "gift of reconciliation" components at exactly the moment the Council needed them. Then leaving. Then waiting. The Company's plan was to offer restoration services within six months. The Bloom has been more persistent than their models predicted.

## The Personnel

**[[Saleh]]** — Field agent, players' direct contact. He is not a senior Company operative. He was given a simple brief: hire capable people, get the scroll, don't make scenes. He genuinely doesn't know the full scope of the operation. He believes the Company wants the scroll for "research." He is not actively complicit in what he doesn't know — and he has enough genuine decency that if players explain the full truth to him, he freezes. He doesn't know what to do with that.

**Harun al-Noor** — Factor. Not present. Left Issyldir 21 months ago. Players can find his correspondence in [[The Amber House|the Amber House's]] archive. He is methodical and unsentimental. His letters are not villainous — they're professional. That's the point.

**Yasmeen al-Bakri** — Junior agent, still in the Amber House. See [[The Amber House]] for her full profile. She is the Company's human face — and its most convenient point of accountability.

**The Company's leadership** — Not present, not named. The adventure does not resolve their storyline. They exist as a structural force, not characters.

---

## What They Want

From the adventure: the Sealed Scroll, extracted from [[Parveen|Parveen]], delivered to [[Saleh|Saleh]].

From [[Issyldir - The City|Issyldir]], long-term: a proof-of-concept that a targeted Bloom event can be engineered and contained. They need to know the ritual can be controlled before deploying it elsewhere.

**The terrible irony**: The Bloom in Issyldir is proof the ritual *cannot* be controlled. The Gardener's arrival was not planned. The Company's models did not predict a primordial entity using their sabotage as an entry point. They wanted a slow economic disruption, not an apocalypse. Their plan worked too well and in the wrong direction.

## If Players Confront [[Saleh]] with the Evidence

[[Saleh]] has three reactions available depending on trust level and how the confrontation is handled:

1. **Low trust / hostile delivery**: He runs. He needs to report to his handlers. This is above his authority level.
2. **Medium trust / civil delivery**: He listens. Long silence. *"If what you're telling me is true..."* He does not complete the sentence. He looks at the scroll in his hands (if he has it) and puts it down. He does not pick it up again.
3. **High trust / built relationship**: He helps. He provides the Company's communication channel and agrees to submit the archive evidence to an external authority. He cannot make the Company face consequences — he is a field agent — but he can delay, complicate, and obstruct.

## The Players' Employer Problem

The adventure begins with players working for the Amber Company. By the third encounter, they should have enough information to understand who they work for and what the delivery of the scroll will enable.

**The Clue Trail** tracks when players gain access to each piece of evidence. At full information:
- The Amber Company sabotaged the ritual deliberately
- They want the scroll to repeat this process in another city
- Their field agent (Saleh) may not know this
- Delivering the scroll gives them that capacity

Players must decide what they're working for. The adventure does not punish them for delivering the scroll — but it makes the implications visible.
